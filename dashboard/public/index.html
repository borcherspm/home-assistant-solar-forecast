🌲 Why Aug 21 Hit 34.0 kWh, Why Sunny Sept Days Stalled at 24–27 kWh, and What Happened on Sept 23

On Aug 21 (first day of allowed exporting), the array produced 34.0 kWh—even though the tree was already shading the panels from 1:00 PM to 4:00 PM (clipping 2 PM down to 2.59 kW and 3 PM to 1.84 kW before rebounding to 2.34 kW at 5 PM once the sun passed the tree!). Why did Aug 21 reach 34 kWh while sunny days in September (Sept 4, 6, 7, 11, 12, 14, 15, 22) only made 24–27.8 kWh? Because in late August, days were 13.65 hours long with higher solar elevation, adding ~7 kWh in the early morning and late afternoon outside the tree's shadow. Without the tree, Aug 21 would have produced ~43.2 kWh! As the sun dropped lower in September, the tree shadow bit deeper and longer into the afternoon until the tree was felled by noon on Sept 23—instantly rocketing afternoon (12–4 PM) output from ~11 kWh to 20.3 kWh and lifting daily output back to 34.2 kWh!

🗄️ Append-Only Versioned SQLite Storage (`dashboard/data/solar_forecasts.db` • `forecast_versions`)

Zero overwrites: Every forecast is stored as an immutable versioned row (v1, v2, v3...) in the forecast_versions SQLite table. Whenever Open-Meteo updates a future day's weather (or a new day starts), the server automatically inserts a new version (vN + 1) with the exact timestamp, lead-time hours, and change reason—so you can see both how the weather forecast shifted over time and how each version compared to final Home Assistant actuals!
